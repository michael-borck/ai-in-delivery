# Facilitator Quick Reference: AI in Delivery (revamped)

**One-day flow. One project, carried all the way through. Print this.**

---

## The spine (say it out loud at the start)

They came to find out **why AI is different**. The answer is the **five differences**: (1) "done" can't be specified, (2) the demo is a trap, (3) data is discovered not specified, (4) verification is the product, (5) generic competence is the baseline. Everything today hangs off these.

Set expectations early (fixes the old #1 critique): *"This is a leadership/delivery course. You'll use AI, but the skill is judgement."*

## Groups & projects

- 14–15 people → **4 groups of ~4** (or 3 of 5).
- **Each group takes a different initiative** so AI-assisted outputs don't all converge: customer-service chatbot / dynamic pricing / inventory optimisation / fraud detection. (3 groups → drop fraud detection.)
- Self-marked against the worksheets. The debrief is each group teaching the others about a different failure profile.

## The chatbots (afternoon spine)

- **Baseline (always works):** if bots aren't live, hand out the staff profiles as briefing cards; groups interrogate them on paper. This is the proven speed-dating format.
- **Upgrade (the wow):** live RetailFlow virtual-staff bots at retailflow.eduserver.au. Even 1–2 live for the morning demo gives the wow.
- Bots disagree with each other on purpose. Marcus = speed; Priya = data realism; David = certainty/ROI; Emma = board promise; Tom = frontline impact; Sarah = operational reality.

## Pacing note (from prior feedback)

Use a **visible timer**. Discussion ran long last time, so keep sprints tight and protect the debriefs.

## Delivery note on the inserts

Keep the apprenticeship/evaluator material conceptual. **Don't quote model versions or precise study stats from memory.** If citing, name OpenAI's **GDPval** and say "recent studies suggest."

## Capstone line

*"Notice how generic your AI-assisted first draft was. That sameness is the lesson. What made a plan good was your judgement: where you kept a human, what you refused to automate, the taste you protected. When everyone runs the same model, that's the only edge left."*
